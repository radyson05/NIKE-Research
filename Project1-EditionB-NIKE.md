## Operating-value driver: NIKE Direct revenue and customer traffic

NIKE Direct revenue was **$17.7 billion** in fiscal 2026, compared with **$18.8 billion** in fiscal 2025: a reported decline of 6% and a currency-neutral decline of 8%. The filing attributes this to a 12% decline in NIKE Brand Digital sales and a 4% decline in NIKE store sales. NIKE states that the Digital decline was primarily due to reduced traffic.

Continued traffic weakness could limit revenue growth. If NIKE relies on greater promotional discounts or marketing spending to attract customers, it may also pressure margins and operating cash flow. Further research should determine whether the traffic decline is temporary or persistent, what caused it, and whether Direct sales can recover without reliance on discounts or incremental marketing spend.

Source: FY2026 Form 10-K, "Fiscal 2026 NIKE Brand Revenue Highlights," p. 33.

## Additional MD&A evidence

### Revenue mix and geographic performance

**Nike-reported facts.** Total fiscal-2026 revenue was $46,398 million, essentially flat from $46,309 million in fiscal 2025; on a currency-neutral basis, revenue declined 2%. NIKE Brand wholesale revenue increased 6% to $27,453 million. North America revenue increased 5% to $20,511 million and North America EBIT increased 14% to $5,376 million. In contrast, Greater China revenue declined 11% reported and 13% on a currency-neutral basis to $5,847 million, while Greater China EBIT declined 20% to $1,278 million. Nike states that negative effects in Greater China are expected to continue through fiscal 2027.

**Analysis.** The channel and regional results are uneven: North American wholesale strength supports revenue, but it does not establish a recovery in the Direct channel already discussed above. Greater China remains a material constraint on consolidated revenue growth and profitability; a later valuation should test the timing and economics of a regional recovery.

Sources: [FY2026 Form 10-K, "Fiscal 2026 Financial Highlights," p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); ["Fiscal 2026 NIKE Brand Revenue Highlights," pp. 32-33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); ["North America," p. 37, and "Greater China," p. 39](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm).

### Margin, cash flow, and liquidity

**Nike-reported facts.** Consolidated gross margin increased 20 basis points to 42.9% in fiscal 2026. Nike attributes the improvement to lower warehousing and logistics costs, favorable foreign-exchange effects including hedges, and lower other costs; higher NIKE Brand product costs and lower Converse margin partly offset these benefits. Cash provided by operations declined to $2,868 million from $3,698 million in fiscal 2025. Nike attributes the fiscal-2026 working-capital outflow primarily to higher accounts receivable, including an IEEPA tariff receivable, and lower income taxes payable. As of May 31, 2026, cash and equivalents plus short-term investments totaled approximately $9.0 billion.

**Analysis.** The gross-margin improvement is constructive, but its stated drivers include channel mix and foreign exchange, so it should not be assumed to be fully structural. The decline in operating cash flow makes receivables and working-capital normalization relevant to any future free-cash-flow forecast. The liquidity balance provides near-term financial flexibility, but it does not by itself resolve the operating issues described above.

Sources: [FY2026 Form 10-K, "Gross Margin" and "Total Selling and Administrative Expense," p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); ["Liquidity and Capital Resources," pp. 45-46](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm).

### Currency, commitments, and inventory-estimate sensitivity

**Nike-reported facts.** Foreign-exchange translation increased reported fiscal-2026 revenue by approximately $1,023 million and income before income taxes by approximately $184 million. Nike disclosed approximately $15.5 billion of endorsement-contract obligations, $4.9 billion of product-purchase obligations, and $2.4 billion of other-purchase obligations as of May 31, 2026. Nike also states that inventory reserves depend on estimates of future demand, market conditions, inventory levels, and sales trends; a decline in estimated net realizable value would require a higher reserve.

**Analysis.** Currency-neutral comparisons are important when evaluating Nike's underlying growth. The disclosed obligations are cash commitments to monitor in a later cash-flow analysis, rather than financial debt under the debt definition used above. Demand, discounting, and inventory-reserve assumptions are particularly relevant while Direct traffic is weak.

Sources: [FY2026 Form 10-K, "Foreign Currency Exposures and Hedging Practices," pp. 43-44](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); ["Liquidity and Capital Resources," p. 46](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); ["Critical Accounting Estimates - Inventory Reserves," p. 47](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm).
